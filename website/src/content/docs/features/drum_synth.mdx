---
title: Drum Synth
---

The drum synth is an oscillator type for synthesised drums. It is a port of the drum voices from the open source LXR-02 firmware by Erica Synths, adapted to run inside a Deluge sound. The Deluge's own envelopes, filters, FX, mod matrix, automation and parameter locks all work on top of it.

It has seventeen engines and variants, all chosen in **SYNTH MODE**. The first three are ported from the LXR-02:

- **Kick / tom**: a main oscillator with a pitch envelope, plus a mod oscillator that either frequency modulates it or is mixed with it.
- **Snare**: a pitched oscillator with a pitch envelope, plus sample-and-hold noise through its own low, high or band pass filter.
- **Cymbal / hat**: a main oscillator frequency modulated by two more oscillators, one of them usually noise.

**DXIA** and **DIIA** (three variants each) come from the XIA engine set made for the Elektron Model:Cycles. See [DXIA and DIIA](#dxia-and-diia). The eight **Cyc engines** are modelled on the Model:Cycles machines. See [Cyc engines](#cyc-engines). **GIN** is the PO-32 style drum voice from SquareWave Studio's GIN for the OP-1, with sixteen built in eight-sound kits. See [GIN](#gin).

Every LXR voice also has a **transient**: a pitch "snap", a start-phase "offset" click, or one of twelve short click samples.

## Making a drum synth kit

1. In clip view on an empty clip, press **SHIFT + KIT**.
2. The **New kit** menu opens. Turn **SELECT** to a kit type and press **SELECT** (or **KIT**):
   - **Kit**: a normal, sample based kit.
   - **LXR**: seven LXR voice rows, from the bottom: **KICK**, **SNARE**, **CH** (closed hat), **OH** (open hat), **LTOM**, **HTOM** and **CYM**. The two hat rows are in choke mode, so an open hat is cut off by the next closed hat.
   - **Cycles**: the Model:Cycles' usual six tracks as Cyc engine rows, from the bottom: **KIK**, **SNR**, **METAL**, **TONE**, **PERC** and **CHORD**. They play at the note, as the Cycles does at its default tune.
   - **GIN**: eight GIN rows, from the bottom: **KICK**, **SNR**, **HAT1**, **HAT2** (choked by HAT1), **PERC1**, **PERC2**, **CLAP** and **OTHER**, playing GIN's first built in kit (808). Each row is tuned to its sound.

Picking **LXR**, **Cycles** or **GIN** goes straight into that kit's [Kit presets](#kit-presets) (from v1.19), playing the top one: the first preset in its folder, or the kit as described above if the folder is empty (on GIN, its first built in kit). Keep it with **SELECT**, or **BACK** to the New kit menu with the kit as described above.

The rows a preset kit makes have softly coloured audition pads, by the engine each plays: blue for the Cyc engines, orange for the LXR voices, green for DXIA and DIIA, pink for GIN (brighter on the selected row). You can add more rows above or below them: tap an empty audition pad and a new row appears there with **SYNTH MODE** open, so you can turn it into any engine, **Sample** or FM straight away (or hold the empty pad and press **LOAD** for a sample, **SYNTH** for a synth preset, **MIDI** or **CV**, as on any kit). Added rows aren't coloured. On a preset kit, **SHIFT + KIT** opens its [Kit presets](#kit-presets); **BACK** from there goes up to the Kit / LXR / Cycles / GIN menu, where its own type goes back into its presets. Once a preset kit has rows added, that menu won't swap it for another kit, which would leave the added rows without their sounds: the screen shows **CANT**. Without added rows, swapping keeps the pattern on the rows the new kit fills.

Every other engine (Vtg Snr, clap eng, DXIA, DIIA) is a **SYNTH MODE** choice. You can turn any row of any kit, or any synth, into a drum synth: press the **SYNTH MODE** shortcut and choose an engine. That way sampled and synthesised drums can share a kit. See [Choosing an engine](#choosing-an-engine).

## Kit presets

A preset kit (LXR, Cycles or GIN) can swap all its preset rows' sounds at once for a saved preset, with the pattern still playing.

1. On a preset kit, press **SHIFT + KIT**. The **Kit presets** (**PRST**) list opens instead of the New kit menu.
2. Turn **SELECT**. Each preset plays on the kit's preset rows straight away; the pattern, its notes and any rows you added stay as they are. The audition pads still play the rows.
3. Press **SELECT** (or **KIT**) to keep the one showing. **BACK** puts the kit back as it was and goes up to the Kit / LXR / Cycles / GIN menu, where you can swap to the other preset kit (if no rows have been added) or go back into the presets.

**Without the list** (from v1.19): in the clip, turning **SELECT** on a preset kit steps to the next or previous one in the same list and keeps it straight away, with the name on the screen. It goes round at the ends. Turning **SELECT** never swaps a preset kit for a sample kit.

The list is **Default** (the rows as SHIFT + KIT first makes them) then the presets in name order. On a GIN kit, **Default** is replaced by GIN's sixteen built in kits (see [GIN](#gin)), so they can be cycled through with nothing on the card; the one you keep is remembered like a preset. It opens on the one the kit is on, so you carry on from there: the last preset kept (saved with the kit), or the preset a kit loaded from the presets folder is named after. A kit on none of them gets **Current** at the top.

**New kit tracks as a preset kit.** Set **SETTINGS > DEFAULTS > UI > CLIP TYPE > KIT INSTRUMENT** (**KIT**) to **LXR**, **Cycles** or **GIN** (from v1.19; **OFF**, the default, is stock). Then every new kit track (a new clip in song view, the **KIT** button on a clip that has never been a kit, a new arranger track) starts as that kit on its top preset that isn't on another track, or the kit as SHIFT + KIT makes it if there's none. A GIN track starts on GIN's first built in kit.

**Making presets.** A preset is an ordinary kit file in **KITS/CYCLES** (Cycles kits), **KITS/LXR** (LXR kits) or **KITS/GIN** (GIN kits). LXR-02 kits (.SND files) convert with `lxr_to_deluge.py`, Model:Cycles kits with `cycles_to_deluge.py`. Set up a preset kit's rows the way you like, then save the kit into that folder. The folder is made the first time you open the Kit presets list, so it's there to save into. Only the preset rows are read back, bottom first: rows you added to the kit you saved from are ignored. A kit file made by hand with no preset rows gives its sound rows, bottom first. If a preset has fewer rows than the kit, the rest keep their sounds.

Swapping sounds clears the undo history.

## Affect all

Affect all is a performance mode for LXR, Cycles and GIN kits. It takes a temporary snapshot of the kit, lets you push every drum around at once, then puts it all back on the next bar.

1. In a preset kit's clip, press **SHIFT + AFFECT ENTIRE**. The kit is snapshotted and **AFFECT ENTIRE** flashes (**ALL**). You start in **All voices**.
2. **All voices:** turn any gold knob, or open a setting (**SHIFT** + a shortcut pad) and turn **SELECT**, and every drum synth row moves by the same amount, each from where it was. Each row moves its own engine's control on that knob or pad, so on a kit mixing engines (a Cycles kit with a BXIA row added, say) Sweep moves the sweep on every row. A row pushed to the end of a setting keeps its place against the others when you turn back.
3. **Kit FX:** press **AFFECT ENTIRE** on its own to swap to the kit's FX (**FX**), the same as AFFECT ENTIRE normally does, and again to go back to **All voices**. Swap as often as you like: the snapshot holds both.
4. Press **SHIFT + AFFECT ENTIRE** again. **AFFECT ENTIRE** flashes twice as fast (**BACK**), and on the next bar every row and the kit's FX go back to the snapshot, before that bar's first hits. When stopped, it goes back straight away (**DONE**).

- Only drum synth rows move, including ones added to the kit since. Sample rows, synth rows and the 303 are left alone.
- Changes in affect all are never recorded as automation, so the snapshot always puts the kit back exactly. Holding notes and turning a knob still parameter locks that row, as normal.
- Settings that are choices (waves, modes, filter type), patch cable amounts and transpose stay on the one row.
- The snapshot is held in memory only. It isn't saved, and loading another song drops it.
- On any other kit, **SHIFT + AFFECT ENTIRE** shows **CANT**.

## Pad editor

On a drum synth row, open the sound menu and choose **Drum synth** (its first item), or press **SELECT** on **OSC1 > TYPE** in the sound menu. The pad editor opens: every pad column is one setting, shown as a level meter from the bottom row (lowest) to the top row (highest).

- **Tap a pad** to set that column to that level. The screen shows the setting's name and value.
- **Turn SELECT** to fine tune the column you last touched.
- **Turn the horizontal encoder** to move between columns without changing anything.
- **Audition pads** still play the row, so you can hold one and tap settings while you listen.
- **SELECT press** opens the full drum synth menu below, with Load preset.
- **BACK** goes up to the normal sound menu. The pad editor is its first item, **Drum synth**.
- **SHIFT** shows the usual grid shortcuts while held.

Single-choice settings (waves, osc mode, noise filter) light one pad per option instead of a meter. The transient column runs from Snap at the bottom, through Offset, to Click 12 at the top.

Columns are coloured by section: white for the main osc, orange for the pitch envelope, light blue for the mod osc and FM, dark blue for the second mod osc, lime for the snare noise, pink for the transient, and green for the Deluge's own controls.

## Drum synth menu

The same settings as a list: press **SELECT** in the pad editor, or go to **OSC1 > TYPE** and press **SELECT**, then **SELECT** again. Settings that the current engine doesn't use are hidden.

| Setting | Engines | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| Load preset | all | Replaces the drum synth settings with a starting point. Leaves the Deluge's envelopes, filters and FX alone |
| Osc wave | all | Main oscillator wave: sine, triangle, saw, square or noise |
| Pitch amount | kick, snare | Depth of the pitch envelope, up to 32 times the base pitch |
| Pitch decay | kick, snare | How fast the pitch falls |
| Pitch curve | kick, snare | Shape of the fall. 64 is linear, lower falls away faster |
| Osc mode | kick | FM: the mod oscillator modulates the main one. Mix: the two are mixed |
| FM amount | kick, cymbal | FM depth, or mod oscillator level in mix mode |
| Mod wave, Mod pitch, Mod fine | kick, cymbal | The mod oscillator. Pitch is in semitones above the main oscillator, 64 = unison |
| FM 2 amount, Mod 2 wave, Mod 2 pitch, Mod 2 fine | cymbal | The second mod oscillator |
| Noise mix | snare | Noise level against the oscillator |
| Noise rate | snare | Sample-and-hold rate of the noise. 127 is plain white noise |
| Noise filter, Noise cutoff, Noise resonance | snare | The snare's own noise filter |
| Transient | all | Snap, Offset, or Click 1 to 12 |
| Transient level, Transient speed | all | Level and playback speed of the transient |

Values run 0 to 127, as on the original hardware.

The pitch of a row is set with the usual oscillator transpose and the Deluge's pitch controls, so the drum synth responds to note, pitch automation and the mod matrix like any other oscillator.

## Choosing an engine

Every engine is in one list: press the **SYNTH MODE** shortcut (or **OSC1 TYPE** on a drum synth row, which opens the same list) and turn SELECT:

| Group | Choices |
|---|---|
| The Deluge's own | Sample (on a kit row) or Subtractive, FM, Ringmod, 303 (synths only) |
| LXR voices | Kick / tom, Snare, Cymbal / hat |
| DXIA | DXIA L, DXIA B, DXIA C |
| DIIA | DIIA G, DIIA I, DIIA H |
| Cyc engines | Cyc Kik, Cyc Snr, Cyc Metal, Cyc Perc, Cyc Tone, Cyc Chord, Vtg Snr, clap eng |
| GIN | GIN (starts on the 808 kit's kick; **GIN sound** in the Drum synth menu loads any of the 128 kit sounds) |
| ULTRAKIK | ULTRAKIK, the kick engine (new in v1.21, see [ULTRAKIK](#ultrakik)) |

Choose **Sample** to put a kit row back on its sample.

Each engine you pick starts from its defaults, set up as its kit row is, so what you hear while choosing is what the kit plays: the transpose, both envelopes, filters (open, with the high pass on for the cymbal), osc 2 and noise off, and the tune centred, whatever the row was before. GIN is tuned to its sound, and like every engine its own envelopes shape the hit inside an open Deluge envelope with the release fitted to its decay. FX are left alone. A row that wasn't a drum synth also gets envelope 2 as a short blip for patching to pitch.

Every drum engine shapes its hits with its own decay: the LXR voices' Decay, DXIA and DIIA's engine decay, the Cyc engines' amp decay. The Deluge envelope around it is the same on every drum row: attack 0, sustain full, and a release long enough for the engine's decay to ring out after the note ends (40, up to 50 for the longest decays). So one tap plays the whole hit, whatever the note length, and gated sounds follow the note. Snappy decay starts off. Envelope 2's decay is 5 unless a preset sets it.

**DXIA and DIIA presets.** Each variant starts on a preset: DXIA's as kicks, DIIA's as noisy percussion, level with the Cyc and LXR engines.

| Variant | Sound | How |
|---|---|---|
| DXIA L | Tight kick | Clean sine at F1, envelope 2 on pitch drops it from about 200 Hz, decay 18, Punch |
| DXIA B | Knocky kick | Operator 1 feeding the chain for a knock, decay 16 |
| DXIA C | Long deep kick | E1, a slower drop, decay 26, more drive |
| DIIA G | Snare-ish noise perc | Loud strike over the partials, decay 9 |
| DIIA I | Zap | The falling partials under the strike, decay 10 |
| DIIA H | Metallic noise | Chained sines, bright, decay 8 |

Picking another DXIA or DIIA variant starts that variant's preset.

## Shortcut pads

Every drum engine has the same settings on the same pads, in the **MOD1** and **MOD2** columns (changed in v1.16). Hold **SHIFT** and press a pad to open its setting. The same pad does the same job on every engine: decay on one engine's decay pad is decay on all of them. An engine without a setting leaves its pad empty.

On a 7-segment Deluge, while you hold a shortcut pad the display scrolls the full name of what it changes, and when you let go it shows the value (new in v1.20). This works on every shortcut pad, not just the drum synth ones.

- **MOD1, from the bottom up:** Decay, Drive, Pitch, Pitch envelope amount, Pitch envelope speed, Character A, Character B, Extra 1.
- **MOD2, from the bottom up:** Extras 2 to 9, each engine's own settings.
- **OSC1 TYPE** opens SYNTH MODE, where the engine is chosen.
- **OSC1, from PULSE WIDTH up (ULTRAKIK only):** an engine with more settings than fit on MOD1 and MOD2 puts them on the OSC1 pads a drum synth has no use for: PULSE WIDTH, RETRIGGER and FEEDBACK, with ULTRAKIK's presets on WAVE INDEX. OSC1 LEVEL, TRANSPOSE, TYPE and NOISE keep their jobs.
- Every other pad keeps its usual Deluge job: OSC1 LEVEL and TRANSPOSE, OSC2 (a real second oscillator on a drum row), the envelopes, filters, FX and the mod FX pads. The few whose Deluge setting a drum engine uses for its own controls (osc 1 pulse width, both oscillators' feedback and wave index) do nothing on a drum row.
- **Pitch** is the oscillator's transpose, the same pad as OSC1 TRANSPOSE.
- Each pair of gold knobs sits in one column: Drive / Decay, Pitch envelope speed / amount and Character A / B (see Gold knobs below).

Sweep and Contour sit on the pitch envelope's pads on every Cyc engine, though on Cyc Tone, Cyc Chord and clap eng they shape something else (see [Cyc engines](#cyc-engines)).

Settings with no pad are in the sound menu's **Drum synth** item: Mod 2 fine on the Cymbal / hat, and GIN's EQ freq, EQ gain and level.

Every engine's continuous settings are Deluge parameters, so they can be parameter locked, automated, MIDI learned, put on gold knobs and modulated from the mod matrix. They run 0-127, as on the LXR and the Cycles. The choices (waves, transient type, noise filter, osc mode, Punch) are row settings. The full list is in the sound menu's **Drum synth** item.

**Drive and decay.** Every engine has both, on the same pads and gold knobs. DXIA and DIIA use their own. On the LXR voices drive is a soft clipper that gets louder as it goes up and hard clips at the top, and decay is the voice's own fade (127 holds). Both start neutral, so older kits sound the same. The Cyc engines start with drive at 100.

The Cymbal / hat, DXIA and DIIA have no pitch envelope, so their mod rate / depth knobs keep the Deluge's usual LFO rate and vibrato. On the Cyc engines mod depth is now Sweep and mod rate Contour (before v1.16 it was the other way round), and Drive is the engine's drive, not Punch: 0 is clean, 100 is where it starts, 127 is full saturation. With Pad Guides off, the drum knobs keep the v1.11 layout (Cymbal / hat: transient speed / level; DXIA: Blendex 2 / 3; DIIA: Shape / Strike; Cyc: Sweep / Contour on rate / depth). The knobs are set whenever a sound loads or changes engine, so after switching Pad Guides, reload the song.

**303.** A synth in the 303 synth mode follows the same layout: attack / release = **Drive / Amp decay**, mod rate / mod depth = the filter envelope's **Decay / Env mod**, Custom 1's portamento knob = **Slide time**, Custom 2 / Custom 3 = **Accent / Saw-square**. Cutoff and resonance stay on the filter knobs. Its pads match too: OSC1 RETRIGGER accent, PULSE WIDTH saw-square (a morph), FEEDBACK drive, WAVE INDEX amp decay, MOD FX RATE / DEPTH decay / env mod. Amp decay runs from 60 ms through Open303's own 1230 ms at the centre to holding at the top. From v1.22.1 the Deluge's LPF MORPH pad is Drive too, ENV1 DECAY is amp decay and ENV2 DECAY is the filter envelope's decay.

**Decay (changed in v1.22.1)** sets how much of the filter envelope is left at the end of a 16th at 130 bpm, from 3% at the bottom (about 30 ms) to 96% at the top (about 2.8 s), so it changes a pattern evenly across the knob. On the 303 panel's 200 to 2000 ms, everything from the middle up sounded the same on 16ths. It acts as you turn it, and accented notes follow it at half the time (200 ms at the default, as on a 303). It shapes the filter only, so it needs some Env mod to be heard. Songs saved before v1.22.1 keep their knob position, which now gives a different time.

**303 filter (new in v1.22).** On a 303 the LPF MODE pad and menu pick the 303's own filter instead of the Deluge's: **303** (Open303's model of the TB-303 ladder), **LP18** (Open303's plainer 18 dB lowpass) or **DIRT**. DIRT is the 303 ladder with a soft clipper where the note meets the resonance, so high resonance squashes and growls instead of ringing clean, with a little lopsided grit. A new 303 starts on DIRT, and from v1.22.1 so does every 303 saved before v1.22.

**Drive (changed in v1.22.1)** is an overdrive after the filter, on all three filters, like a pedal after a 303. It runs oversampled so it doesn't alias. The lows are cut going into the clipper and put back after it, so the bass stays full without turning to mush. The clip is soft and a little lopsided, accents bite first, and a tone lowpass closes from 12 kHz to 4 kHz as it goes up to keep the fizz down. Up to 30 dB goes in, and the level comes out about 3 dB louder at the top, with the resonance's squelch kept. 0 is clean. In v1.22 Drive pushed DIRT's own clipper, which squashed the resonance and got much louder; DIRT's grit now stays at its drive-0 amount.

Custom 2 / Custom 3 never fall back to the Deluge's decimation and bitcrush on a drum synth row. The other gold knobs keep their usual Deluge jobs. Hold a step and turn a knob to parameter lock it.

**Snappy decay.** On any drum synth row, **Snappy decay** (in the sound menu, under **Drum synth**) makes the amp envelope's decay and release follow the square of the Deluge's usual curve: at every point it has dropped twice as many dB, so the same decay setting sounds about half as long, with a tighter body. It starts off on new drum synth rows (each engine's own decay does the shaping), is saved with the row, and only changes envelope 1.

**Envelope 2.** Every drum synth row starts with envelope 2 as a short blip: attack 0, decay 5, sustain 0, release 0. Patch it to pitch (or osc 1 pitch) for a transient snap.

The LXR settings share parameters between engines: each voice's settings are stored with the row and come back when you switch back. **Modulation.** Any mod source can be patched to any of the settings: hold the source's pad with the setting open, as for any Deluge parameter. Each voice gets its own modulation, so velocity, random or an envelope can change every hit. A cable at 50 with its source at full moves a setting 64 steps, from the middle to either end.

**Automation view.** On a drum synth row the MOD1 and MOD2 columns follow the drum synth's own pads, so each setting is where its shortcut is. Pads holding a choice or the pitch are blank, and so are the drum controls' usual Deluge pads elsewhere.

## Pad Guides

**Pad Guides** (**GUID**) is a Community Features setting, on by default (new in v1.16).

- **SHIFT glow.** In a clip on a drum synth (the synth, or the kit's selected row), holding **SHIFT** tints the MOD1 / MOD2 pads that have a setting soft pink (and ULTRAKIK's OSC1 pads), blended over the pad's own colour so notes still show. Press one and the pads stay lit while you edit the setting in the sound menu. Release SHIFT on the clip and they go back. There's no glow with AFFECT ENTIRE on in a kit, where SHIFT + pad edits the kit's FX.
- **Gold knobs.** Drum synth rows use the layout above.
- **Mod button pads.** Holding any gold knob button still shows its two settings' names, and now also flashes their shortcut pads: red for the top knob's, yellow for the bottom's. This works on every sound type, and on kit, audio clip and song FX. A setting with no pad (a learned MIDI CC, most patch cables) just shows its name. The flashing stops when you let go.

## DXIA and DIIA

DXIA and DIIA are ported from the compiled Model:Cycles firmware they were written for. At the Cycles' own sample rate and block size they produce the original's output sample for sample, which the unit tests check.

- **DXIA**: three sine operators and a carrier. Each operator's **Blendex** moves it from being heard (0) to modulating (127). L: all three modulate the carrier. B: a chain, 1 into 2 into 3 into the carrier. C: a loop, 1 into 2 into 3 and back into 1, with no carrier.
- **DIIA**: inharmonic partials. G adds six, I adds six with a falling pitch at the start, H chains eight sines.
- **Freq 1-3**: each operator's tuning, from 24 semitones down at 0, through unison at 64, to 24 up at 127.
- **Weight** picks DIIA's partial ratios, **Alloy** their levels (or the modulation depth on H), and **Strike** the level of a noise burst at the start of each hit.
- **Engine decay** is the engine's own decay, on top of the Deluge's envelope. It starts at 127, which holds (about 9 seconds on DIIA), so the Deluge envelope does the shaping. Turning it down brings back the Cycles' own decay, including DIIA H's brightness fading with it.

**Differences from the Cycles.**
- The Deluge's envelope shapes the level, and the engines' own low pass is left open: use the Deluge's filters.
- Engine decay and other once-per-block changes run about 9% slower, because the Deluge runs at 44.1 kHz rather than 48 kHz. Pitch is exact.
- Blendex 3 runs 0 to 127 like Blendex 1 and 2. On the Cycles it sits on the fine tune slot and mirrors around 64.
- DIIA's attack setting is split: below the middle it set the strike level, which is **Strike** here, and above it a fade-in, which is the Deluge envelope's attack.

## Cyc engines

**Cyc Kik**, **Cyc Snr**, **Cyc Metal**, **Cyc Perc**, **Cyc Tone**, **Cyc Chord**, **Vtg Snr** and **clap eng** are named after the Model:Cycles machines (and two Syntakt machines moved into it by Modded-Cycles) that they are modelled on, and they keep those machines' knob names and starting values.

They are new code, not ports. They were written from general synthesis principles and tuned against renders of the original machines: pitch sweeps, decay times, harmonic content and levels were measured knob by knob and matched. They sound close, not identical.

| Engine | Knob 1 | Knob 2 | Knob 3 | Knob 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cyc Kik | Color: odd harmonic drive | Shape: rounder, then spiky and buzzy, then noisy around 96 | Sweep: pitch drop depth and speed | Contour: length of the pitch drop, short blip to long boom, plus the knock at the start |
| Cyc Snr | Color: rattle tuning | Shape: rattle from tonal to noise | Sweep: body pitch drop | Contour: how fast the noise dies, leaving a tone |
| Cyc Metal | Color: spread of the partials | Shape: density | Sweep: pitch drop | Contour: how fast the top end dies |
| Cyc Perc | Color: bright overtone, 3 to 7 times the note | Shape: modulation, hollow to gnarly | Sweep: pitch drop | Contour: how fast the overtone dies, and the length of the pitch drop |
| Cyc Tone | Color: FM ratio, knob / 12 (24 = 2) | Shape: FM index | Sweep: index burst at the start | Contour: how fast the index fades |
| Cyc Chord | Color: voicing (unison, close, inversions, dropped, spread) | Shape: the chord, 38 of them | Sweep: wave, sine to hollow to buzzy | Contour: how fast the wave mellows |
| Vtg Snr | Inharm: body ratio | Freq complex: snares against body | Pitch sweep | Mod envelope: knock in the body |
| clap eng | Body char: brightness and body resonance | Balance: tail against claps | Spacing crunch: gap between claps, then grit | Body envelope: how fast the tail darkens |

The Shape knob on Cyc Chord runs 0 to 127 like the others, stepping through the 38 chords in the Cycles' order: root and fifth, unison, minor, major, sus2, sus4, m7, 7, m(maj7), maj7, 7sus4, dim7, m(add9), add9, m6, 6, and on through the altered chords to fourths and fifths.

**Punch** (MOD1 top pad) squares up and lifts the attack and stretches the decay a little.

**Level and defaults.** Each engine is levelled to start close to full scale, then driven: **Drive** starts at 100, a soft clipper after the amp decay that gets louder as it goes up, pushing into hard clipping at 127, and cleaning up as the sound dies away. The amp decay starts longer than the original machines' (for example 50 on Cyc Kik, 60 on Cyc Snr, 80 on Cyc Chord).

**Differences from the originals.**
- Same knobs, same starting values, new sound engines inside: the curves behind each knob were matched by measurement, and some knobs do more than the original's (Cyc Kik's Contour barely did anything on the Cycles; here it sets the length of the pitch drop and the knock).
- They run natively at 44.1 kHz, so pitch and decay times are exact.
- No machine-wide gate: notes end with the Deluge envelope.

## GIN

**GIN** is the drum voice from SquareWave Studio's GIN for the original OP-1, which builds on Eric Lewis's libpo32, a PO-32 style drum synth. One voice is a tone and a noise layer:

- **Tone**: a sine, triangle or saw, with its own attack and decay, and a pitch modulator: a decaying **sweep**, a **sine** LFO (at audio rates it frequency modulates the tone, for bells, cowbells and metal), or filtered **noise**.
- **Noise**: white noise through a low, high or band pass filter (or none), with its own attack and decay, and an envelope that is exponential, linear or **modulated**: a decaying train of bursts, for claps.
- **Mix** pans between the two (0 tone only, 64 both, 127 noise only), then GIN's **drive**, a peak **EQ** and its **level**, before a clipper.

Its sixteen built in kits are new for the Deluge (GIN's own factory kits aren't included). Each has eight sounds, on the rows KICK, SNR, HAT1, HAT2, PERC1, PERC2, CLAP and OTHER:

| Kit | Style | Other |
|---|---|---|
| 808 | Classic analogue | Cowbell |
| 909 | Punchy, driven kick | Rim shot |
| ELEC | Electro: zaps, FM hats | Laser |
| MINI | Minimal, short and clicky | Bleep |
| INDU | Industrial, everything driven | Noise-bent saw |
| DUST | Lo-fi, tops rolled off | Rim tick |
| RUMB | Techno rumble, long low noise tail, FM ride | Sub boom |
| ZAP | Fast deep sweeps, pews | Wobble blip |
| BOOM | Trap: long tuned 808, crisp tops | 808 tom |
| TITE | Tight and woody | Side stick |
| METL | Metallic: FM bells and gongs | Cowbell |
| DUB | Deep and round, syndrums | Falling syndrum |
| BLIP | Glitch: bleeps and broken noise | Noise-bent triangle |
| BRKS | Breakbeat crunch | Crash |
| PERC | Hand percussion: frame drum, shakers, congas | Clave |
| CHIP | 8-bit: triangle kick, raw noise | Coin |

Cycle through them with **SHIFT + KIT** on a GIN kit (see [Kit presets](#kit-presets)), or put any one sound on a row with **GIN sound** in the Drum synth menu.

**Settings.** The gold knobs follow the shared layout: attack / release = **Drive / Decay**, mod rate / depth = the pitch modulation's **Mod rate / Mod amount** (64 is none; above 64 the sweep starts high and falls, below it starts low and rises), Custom 1 = tune, Custom 2 / 3 = **Mix / Noise freq**, as on GIN's own front panel. **Decay** moves the tone and noise decays together: 64 leaves them as the sound has them, 0 is about 60 times shorter, 127 about 60 times longer. **Tone decay**, **Noise decay**, both attacks, **Noise reso**, the waves and modes, the EQ and **GIN level** are in the Drum synth menu and the pad editor, and all but the EQ and level on the shortcut pads. The continuous ones are parameters (lockable, automatable, modulatable), EQ freq included from v1.14; the waves, modes, filter type, EQ gain and level are row settings.

**Differences from GIN on the OP-1.**
- The pitch is the note (and row transpose), not a setting: the kits tune each row to its sound.
- Velocity sets level through the Deluge's usual velocity patch, not GIN's per-layer velocity settings.
- There's no A / B morph: parameter locks and automation do that job.
- Settings follow the knobs while a hit plays, where GIN fixes them at the hit.
- A hit ends once both layers are 72 dB down (80 dB before v1.17).

## ULTRAKIK

**ULTRAKIK** (new in v1.21) is a kick engine. It starts as a thick, low thump with colour and grit already on and a short tail, and bends into long gliding 808s, hard 909s and fully broken kicks. It's synthesised, not a sample: put a sample on OSC2 if you want one on top.

**How it works.** One oscillator, swept down from above by two pitch envelopes: **Punch**, a very fast spike of up to four octaves over a few milliseconds (the beater hitting the skin), and **Sweep**, a slower drop of up to five octaves (the boom). **Bend** then moves the pitch slowly over the hit, up to two octaves either way, for 808 slides and drops. **Snap** sets the phase it starts on: the further round, the harder the first edge hits. After the oscillator comes the amp (**Hold** at full level, then **Decay**), a sine wavefolder (**Fold**), the **Drive** stage, **Grit** riding on the driven peaks, the **Click**, the **Crush**er, and finally **Sub**: a clean sine of the body mixed under all of it, so the low end survives whatever you do above it. The amp sits before the drive, like a VCA into a pedal, so more drive keeps the body full for longer.

Picked in SYNTH MODE it starts on its default preset, ULTRA. On a kit row it plays at C2 (about 65 Hz) from the kit's middle C. On a synth it plays at the note.

| Setting | Range | Starts at | What it does |
|---|---|---|---|
| Decay | 40 ms to 8 s to fall 60 dB, 127 holds | 52 (about 0.6 s) | How long the hit rings. At 127 it holds while the note does, for gated 808s |
| Drive | 0 to 36 dB | 60 (24 dB) | Gain into the drive stage. 0 is clean |
| Pitch | Transpose | C2 on a kit row | The note the kick lands on |
| Sweep | None to 5 octaves | 70 | How far above the note the sweep starts |
| Sweep time | 2 ms to 250 ms | 40 | How fast it falls: short for a tight 909, long for a boomy drop |
| Fold | Clean to many folds | 20 | A little rounds and fattens the body, more folds it into bright, BIA-style harmonics |
| Grit | 0 to 127 | 25 | Darkened noise riding on the body's peaks: the rattle of a speaker pushed too far |
| Punch | None to 4 octaves | 60 | The very fast pitch spike at the start |
| Sub | 0 to 127 | 45 | Clean sine under the driven body |
| Hold | 0 to 200 ms | 50 (about 30 ms) | Full level before the decay starts: the thump |
| Click | 0 to 127 | 45 | Transient level |
| Click type | Thud, Tick, Noise, Zap | Thud | Thud: a low passed noise thump. Tick: a short pulse, the 808 / 909 attack. Noise: a bright burst. Zap: a sine falling from kHz in a few ms |
| Click tone | 0 to 127 | 60 | Dark and long to bright and short |
| Bend | 24 semitones down to 24 up, 64 none | 64 | Slow pitch bend over the hit |

The first twelve are parameters (lockable, automatable, modulatable). Crush, Bend time, Snap, Body wave, Drive type and Click type are row settings. Body wave, Snap and Drive type are on the OSC1 pads (PULSE WIDTH, RETRIGGER, FEEDBACK), and the presets on OSC1 WAVE INDEX; everything is in the Drum synth menu, and all but Crush, Bend time and Snap in the pad editor.

**Presets.** Sixteen big kicks, clean and deep at the top of the list, crunchier and more broken further down. Hold SHIFT and press OSC1 WAVE INDEX (or go to **Kick preset** in the Drum synth menu), and turn SELECT: each turn loads the next preset straight away, so you can play the row while you go through them. All sixteen peak within 1 dB of full scale, so moving between them doesn't jump in level.

For a grime or trap 808 line, put ULTRAKIK on a synth with polyphony LEGATO and some portamento, set Decay to 127 (it holds while the note does), and overlap notes to slide.

**Known issue.** On some rows of the Deluge's sample kits ULTRAKIK can stay silent when picked in SYNTH MODE. It plays on rows that are already a drum synth (an LXR, Cycles or GIN kit row) and on most sample kit rows.

**Level.** Every setting peaks at about -1 dB, with a soft knee above that so the click and grit can't push it past full scale. Turning Drive up makes it louder without making it peak higher.

**CPU.** About 1.4 times a Cyc Kik voice at the defaults (under 1% of the CPU per hit), and under 1.8 times with everything on, measured against Cyc Kik on a computer rather than on the Deluge. Each stage runs only while it's doing something: the click stops after its burst, grit once the body is too quiet to carry it, the sweep once it's over. A hit ends once it's 72 dB down, counting the drive's gain.

## Gate out

**Gate out** (**GATE OUT**, new in v1.22) sends a kit's bottom four rows out of the Deluge's four gate outputs as they play: row 1 to gate 1, row 2 to gate 2, row 3 to gate 3, row 4 to gate 4. On the LXR and Cycles preset kits that's kick, snare, hat 1 and hat 2, so a modular or another drum machine can follow the kit.

- **Turning it on.** In the kit's menu (with AFFECT ENTIRE on, after **Kit arp**), or at the end of the **Drum synth** menu on a drum synth row. It's off on new kits and saved with the song.
- **One kit at a time.** Turning it on for one kit turns it off for every other kit in the song.
- **Row gate** (**ROW GATE**, in the **Drum synth** menu after Gate out) picks the gate for that row: **Auto** (by row, as above), **Off**, or gate **1** to **4**. Two rows can share a gate. It's saved with the row.
- **Gate length.** A sound with a tail (a held note, a decay that holds) holds its gate for as long as the note plays. A one-shot hit sends a 12 ms trigger. A new hit drops the gate first, so every hit retriggers, and every gate stays high at least 12 ms so a module always sees it.
- Any sound row in rows 1 to 4 fires a gate, samples included. MIDI and gate rows don't.
- CV clips or a kit's GATE rows that use the same gate outputs still share them with Gate out, so keep them on different gates.

## CPU load

**SHIFT + SELECT > CPU load** shows the audio engine's load while you play:

- **Load**: audio render time as a percentage of real time.
- **Peak**: the highest Load since the page was opened.
- **Voice**: average nanoseconds per sample for one voice, filters and envelopes included.
- **Drum**: average nanoseconds per sample for the drum synth oscillator alone.

One sample lasts 22,676 ns, so a Voice reading of 900 means each playing voice uses about 4% of the CPU. Measured on hardware, a kick / tom drum synth voice costs about as much as a sample voice.

Voice and Drum are only measured while this page is open, so timing them costs nothing the rest of the time.

**How long a hit lasts (from v1.17).** A drum synth voice keeps running until its own decay is 72 dB down, then stops (Cyc engines fade out over the last 64 samples). Before v1.17 they ran to 100 dB down, so long tails (cymbals, open hats, toms, Chord, Tone) kept a voice busy for about a third longer than you could hear. Once a hit is 40 dB down, the Deluge treats it like a releasing note when it runs short of CPU, so it cuts that tail before notes you can still hear. A Decay of 127 still holds for as long as the note does.

**Cyc engines from v1.18.** The Cyc engines build their sines from a lookup table and do their phase modulation in whole numbers, instead of working each sine out in floating point every sample. Metal, Chord, Vtg Snr, Perc and Tone cost about a third to a quarter of what they did, so a Cycles kit and an LXR kit can play together with a 303 and a synth. The sound is the same: the table is accurate to about -106 dB. Metal's chaotic feedback means its waveform never comes out exactly the same from one build to the next, but its sound does.

## Compatibility

Drum synth rows saved by earlier test builds of this drum synth load with their choices intact, but their continuous settings (pitch amount, FM amount and so on) moved onto Deluge parameters: reload a preset or set them again.


Kits that use the drum synth won't play those rows on firmware without it.
